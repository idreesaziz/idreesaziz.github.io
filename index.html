2026LLM evaluation

No One Gets an A: Distributional Bias in LLM Text Scoring

Idrees Aziz, Mahir Sadikhov

Manuscript · First author

We tested whether 11 LLMs could reliably grade writing across approximately 99,000 assessments. Models often gave similar scores to texts of different quality. Across nine models, a scoring correction improved how often better text ranked above worse text from 51.8% to 64.9%.

Paper PDFCode
Methods and my contribution

I designed the experiments, changing grammar, sentence order, word choice, and information separately so their effects could be measured. We compared five score corrections and checked uncertainty with bootstrap confidence intervals and mixed-effects models. Improving the rankings did not necessarily make the models use more of the scoring scale.

2026Learning from crowd beliefs

Recovering Pre-Event Crowd Beliefs in Prediction Markets with a Risk-Calibrated Mixture of Inverse Experts

Idrees Aziz

7th ACM International Conference on AI in Finance (ICAIF 2026)

Accepted · Selected for oral presentation · Proceedings forthcoming, November 2026

Trading can influence the beliefs it is meant to reveal. I built APD-Safe to recover a crowd’s original probability estimate from trading records, using boosted trees to combine several models of trading behaviour. Across 98 real markets, it recovered those beliefs with 10.2% less error than averaging the prices after each trader’s first trade.

Paper PDFPreprint DOI
How I tested it

I compared the recovered beliefs against surveys taken before trading, keeping each research project out of training in turn. The model also reduced reconstruction error by 12.8% across 1,500 simulated markets from ten types of trading behaviour absent from training.

2026Benchmark design

FinEntropyBench: What Does Entropy Tell Us About Market Efficiency?

Idrees Aziz

7th ACM International Conference on AI in Finance (ICAIF 2026)

Accepted · Selected for oral presentation · Proceedings forthcoming, November 2026

Does lower measured randomness mean a market is easier to predict? I built a benchmark comparing entropy measures, an LSTM, and linear and tree-based forecasters across 16 market series. In 113 tests across markets and years, measured patterns did not reliably translate into better direction forecasts or trading profits.

Paper PDFPreprint DOI
What the benchmark separates

I used simulations with known answers to check whether the statistical measures captured what they claimed to measure. The experiments distinguish patterns in the size of price changes from information about whether prices will rise or fall. Models and strategies were selected on earlier data and evaluated on later periods.

2026Synthetic data & transfer learning

Market Decompression: Learning Latent-State Inverse Maps for Cold-Start Financial Prediction

Idrees Aziz

7th ACM International Conference on AI in Finance (ICAIF 2026)

Accepted after peer review · Not included in the proceedings

I trained a Transformer on simulated markets, where hidden volatility and jump events could be used as labels, then adapted it with only five real training examples. Volatility forecast error fell by 4.7% to 7.0% in three of five markets compared with the same model trained without those extra labels.

Paper PDFPreprint DOIEarlier code
Transfer and its limits

A Gaussian-process model identified simulation settings likely to improve real forecasts. I separated that search from confirmation and later test data. Gains did not hold up in the other two markets. The linked code repository documents an earlier cryptocurrency-only version; the PDF above contains the current five-market study.

A public research dataset built from 2,872 Bank of England and Federal Reserve documents. It turns policy text into graphs of linked information, numerical text representations, and tables for ML experiments. Consistency checks and links back to the source documents support verification and reuse.
